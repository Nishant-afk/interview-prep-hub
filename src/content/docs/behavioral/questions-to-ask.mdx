## Why it matters in interviews

*"Do you have any questions for us?"* comes at the end of nearly every round. It is not a formality:

- **Interest and preparation.** A thoughtful, specific question shows you researched the company and are thinking about doing the job, not just getting an offer.
- **Seniority signal.** At 3-4 years, asking about code review culture, on-call and how decisions get made sounds like an engineer who has worked in a real team.
- **Your evaluation.** You are choosing where to spend the next few years. This is your only chance to hear the team's reality before you accept.
- **Last impression.** Ending with "No, I think you covered everything" wastes the final two minutes.

Ask **2-3 questions** per round, tailored to who is in front of you. Do not ask what is answered on the careers page.

## The framework

1. **Prepare 3 per interviewer type** (below), plus 1-2 specific to the company from your research.
2. **Listen during the interview** and ask a follow-up on something they mentioned. This is the strongest type of question.
3. **Ask open questions** ("how", "what", "tell me about"), not yes/no.
4. **Save compensation and leave policy for HR**, not the engineer or hiring manager.

### Template for a researched question

```text
"I read [blog post / release notes / news item] about [specific thing].
How has that affected [the team's work / the architecture / priorities]?"

"You mentioned [something from the interview]. How does the team handle [related challenge]?"
```

## Questions by interviewer type

### Engineer / technical interviewer

| Question | What you learn |
|---|---|
| "What does a typical week look like for a developer on this team?" | Meetings vs coding, support load |
| "How does code get from a PR to production? How often do you deploy?" | CI/CD maturity, release pain |
| "How are code reviews done, and how long does a typical PR wait?" | Engineering culture, bottlenecks |
| "What does the test setup look like: unit, integration, e2e?" | Quality culture, how scary releases are |
| "What is the biggest piece of tech debt right now, and is there a plan for it?" | Honesty, whether debt is managed |
| "How is on-call handled, and how often are you paged?" | Work-life balance, system stability |
| "Which .NET and Angular versions are you on, and how do upgrades happen?" | Whether you will work on modern code |
| "What did you work on last that you were proud of?" | Their enthusiasm, what good work looks like here |

### Hiring manager / engineering manager

| Question | What you learn |
|---|---|
| "What would success look like in this role after 3 and 6 months?" | Expectations, whether the role is well defined |
| "What are the team's biggest challenges this year?" | Real problems you would work on |
| "How are priorities decided between product, engineering and tech debt?" | Who has influence, how much is firefighting |
| "How do people grow from this level to the next? Can you give an example?" | Real promotion path versus vague promises |
| "How is the team structured, and who would I work with most?" | Team size, seniority mix, mentorship |
| "How do you give feedback, and how often are 1:1s?" | Management style |
| "Why is this position open?" | New headcount (growth) versus backfill (find out why the person left) |

### HR / recruiter

| Question | What you learn |
|---|---|
| "What are the next steps and the timeline for a decision?" | Process clarity, when to follow up |
| "How is the compensation structured: fixed, variable, ESOPs, joining bonus?" | Offer components (see [salary & offers](../salary-negotiation/)) |
| "What is the work arrangement: remote, hybrid, office days, location flexibility?" | Practical fit |
| "How is the variable pay calculated, and what was the actual payout last year?" | Whether variable is real money |
| "What is the appraisal cycle, and when would I be eligible for my first one?" | First hike timing |
| "For service companies: how are project allocations decided, and what is the bench policy?" | Risk of unwanted projects or bench time |
| "Is there a notice period buyout or joining bonus to cover it?" | Practical joining arrangements |

## Sample set for one interview loop

<Aside type="tip" title="Sample: adapt to your target company">
For a fictional product company hiring a .NET + Angular SDE-2 for an order platform.
</Aside>

```text
Round 1 (engineer):
  "You mentioned the order service is moving to event-driven processing.
   How are you handling message ordering and duplicate events today?"
  "How long does it take from merging a PR to it running in production?"

Round 2 (hiring manager):
  "What would make someone in this role clearly successful in the first six months?"
  "What's the biggest challenge the team faces this year?"

Round 3 (HR):
  "What are the next steps and when can I expect a decision?"
  "How is variable pay calculated, and what has the typical payout been?"
```

## Red flags to listen for

| What you hear | What it may mean |
|---|---|
| "We're like a family" + "we all pitch in late when needed" | Long hours are normal |
| Vague answer to "how do you deploy?" or "manual deployments every few weeks" | Painful, risky releases |
| No tests, or "QA handles testing" | You will fix regressions in production |
| Can't describe what success looks like | Unclear role, moving goalposts |
| The role has been backfilled several times recently | Retention or management problem |
| Interviewer is dismissive, distracted or late without apology | Possibly the culture you would join |
| "We use the latest tech" but cannot name versions | Buzzwords over reality |
| HR won't explain variable pay or says "it depends" without numbers | Variable may rarely pay out |
| Constant "it's a fast-paced environment" with frequent priority changes | Firefighting culture |

One red flag is not a dealbreaker. Use them to decide what to ask in later rounds, or to ask to speak with a team member before accepting.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask follow-ups on something said in the interview | Ask questions answered on their website |
| Match the question to the interviewer's role | Ask the engineer about salary or leave policy |
| Ask open "how" and "what" questions | Ask yes/no questions |
| Write down the answers right after the round | Forget what each round told you |
| Keep it to 2-3 questions when time is short | Run 10 minutes over the slot |

## Pitfalls

<Aside type="caution" title="Common mistakes">
- **"No questions."** Signals low interest. Always have at least one.
- **Salary and leave in the technical round.** Wrong audience; it suggests misplaced priorities.
- **"Did I pass?" / "How did I do?"** Puts the interviewer on the spot. Ask about next steps instead.
- **Questions that sound like complaints** ("Do you expect people to work weekends?"). Ask neutrally: "How is on-call and release timing handled?"
- **Showing off.** A question designed to display your knowledge rather than learn something comes across as arrogant.
</Aside>

## Follow-up situations

<details>
<summary>The interviewer gives a vague or evasive answer</summary>

Ask once more, politely and more specifically: "Could you give me an example from the last release?" If it is still vague, note it as a possible red flag and check it with another person in a later round.
</details>

<details>
<summary>They ask "What else would you like to know about us?" and you have run out</summary>

Use a personal question: "What do you enjoy most about working here?" or "What made you join?" People like answering these, and the answer tells you a lot.
</details>

<details>
<summary>There are only two minutes left</summary>

Ask one high-value question: "What would success in this role look like in six months?" for a manager, or "What's the biggest technical challenge right now?" for an engineer.
</details>

<details>
<summary>All your prepared questions were answered during the interview</summary>

Say so, and show you were listening: "You've covered most of what I wanted to ask, especially about the deployment process. One thing I'd like to know more about is..."
</details>
