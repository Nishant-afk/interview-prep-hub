## Why it matters in interviews

HR and managerial rounds rarely reject you for being average, but they often reject candidates for **red flags**: badmouthing an employer, unclear motivation, flight risk, or an attitude problem. In Indian service companies the HR round also checks practical fit: notice period, relocation, shifts, bond or service agreements. In product companies a hiring manager uses the same questions to judge motivation and self-awareness.

What they are assessing across all of these:

- **Motivation:** are you moving *towards* something, or just escaping?
- **Stability:** will you stay long enough to be worth the hiring cost?
- **Self-awareness and honesty:** can you talk about weaknesses without spin?
- **Fit:** does what you want match what this role offers?

The framework for almost every answer: **honest + positive + forward-looking + short** (30-60 seconds).

## Why are you looking for a change?

Frame it as growth you want, not problems you are escaping. Even if the real reason is pay or a bad manager, there is always a true, positive version.

```text
"I've learned a lot at [Company], especially [skill/achievement]. I'm now looking for
[more scale / product ownership / design responsibility / a specific domain / tech],
and that's limited in my current role because [neutral, factual reason].
[Target company] offers exactly that through [specific team/product/scale]."
```

**Sample (replace with your own):** "In three and a half years I've gone from building screens to owning modules end to end at TradeLink. The product is stable now and most of our work is incremental features for a few hundred customers. I want to work on systems with larger scale and more design ownership, and your order platform handles a far higher volume, which is the kind of problem I want to solve next."

| Real reason | How to frame it |
|---|---|
| Low pay | "Looking for a role that matches my current scope and responsibility" (and discuss numbers only in the salary conversation) |
| Bad manager / politics | "Looking for a team with strong engineering culture and mentorship" |
| Boring maintenance project | "Want to build new things and own design decisions" |
| Layoff / project ended | State it plainly: "My project was wound down as part of a restructuring that affected [N] people." No shame in it |
| Short tenure (under 1 year) | "The role turned out to be [factual difference from what was offered]. I'm being careful to choose a long-term fit this time" |

## Why this company?

Shows you did your homework. Mention **one product reason, one engineering reason, one personal fit reason**.

```text
"Three things: first, [product/mission fact you researched]. Second, [engineering:
stack, scale, engineering blog post, open-source work]. Third, [fit: this role's
focus on X matches my experience in Y and where I want to grow]."
```

For service companies: talk about the variety of domains and clients, the learning programmes, global delivery exposure and a stable career path. For product companies: the product itself, its users, scale and engineering culture.

## Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths:** pick 2-3 that the role needs, each backed by a 1-line proof. "I'm a hard worker" with no evidence is filler.

```text
"My strongest area is [skill], for example [one-line proof with metric].
I'm also [second strength], which showed when [one-line proof]."
```

**Weaknesses:** pick a **real, non-fatal** weakness, show what you are doing about it and the evidence it is improving. Avoid fake weaknesses ("perfectionist", "I work too hard"); interviewers hear them daily.

```text
"[Real weakness]. For example, [situation where it showed].
I've been working on it by [concrete actions], and [evidence of improvement]."
```

**Sample weakness (replace):** "I used to hold back from speaking up in design discussions with senior architects, even when I had a concern. In one case I didn't raise a doubt about a caching approach and it caused a bug later. Since then I write my concerns in a short note before the meeting and make sure I raise at least the main one. In the last quarter I proposed two design changes that were accepted."

Good real weaknesses for a 3-4 year developer: limited experience with a specific area the role does not depend on (e.g. Kubernetes in production), public speaking, delegating, over-committing to too many tasks, going too deep before asking for help.

## Where do you see yourself in 5 years?

They want ambition that fits the company, not "your job" or "running my own startup".

```text
"In the next [2-3] years I want to become a strong [senior engineer] who
[owns the design of a significant system / mentors others]. Longer term I see
myself as a [tech lead / architect / staff engineer], ideally growing within
a company like [Company], where [reason the path exists there]."
```

## Other common questions

| Question | Framing |
|---|---|
| "Explain this gap in your resume." | One honest sentence (health, family, upskilling, layoff), what you did during it, and that you are ready now. Do not over-explain |
| "Are you open to relocation?" | Answer clearly. If yes, say it. If conditional, state the condition once ("yes, for [city]; for others I'd need to discuss") |
| "Are you comfortable with shifts / client time zones?" | Be honest; a "yes" you cannot keep becomes a problem later |
| "Do you have other offers?" | Truthful and brief: "I'm in final rounds with two companies, but this role is my first preference because..." |
| "Why should we hire you?" | Restate 2-3 matches between their needs and your proof points, under 45 seconds |
| "What is your notice period?" | State it exactly and whether it can be bought out. See [salary & notice period](../salary-negotiation/) |
| "Tell me about a time you failed." | Behavioral: use a [STAR story](../star-stories/) |
| "Why are you switching from a service company to a product company?" (or the reverse) | Product: ownership, depth, long-term product thinking. Service: breadth of domains, client exposure, structured growth |
| "What do you do outside work?" | One or two genuine interests, briefly. A tech community or side project helps but is not required |

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Keep answers to 30-60 seconds | Turn every question into a 5-minute story |
| Be positive about past employers | Criticise your manager, team or company |
| Research the company before the call | Ask "what does your company do?" |
| Give a real weakness with real improvement | Use clichés like "perfectionist" |
| Be clear about notice period and location | Give vague answers you will need to correct later |
| Stay consistent with your resume | Contradict dates or titles from your resume |

## Pitfalls

<Aside type="caution" title="Common mistakes">
- **Saying "only for money"** or "for growth" with nothing behind it. Name the specific growth.
- **Badmouthing.** Even a fair criticism sounds bitter in an interview.
- **Generic "why this company"** that would work for any company.
- **Over-sharing** personal reasons, health details or family issues beyond one sentence.
- **Inconsistency** between rounds, for example telling HR you want to stay technical and the manager you want to become a manager.
- **Treating HR as a formality.** Offers do get dropped at this stage, often for attitude.
</Aside>

## Follow-up questions to expect

<details>
<summary>"But what is the real reason you're leaving?"</summary>

They are probing for a hidden red flag. Repeat your reason calmly with one extra concrete detail. Do not suddenly switch to a negative reason; that looks like your first answer was rehearsed spin.
</details>

<details>
<summary>"What would your current manager say about you?"</summary>

Quote real feedback from appraisals: one strength and one development area. It shows self-awareness and is easy to verify.
</details>

<details>
<summary>"If you get a counter-offer, will you stay?"</summary>

"I'm moving for the role and growth, not just compensation, so a counter-offer wouldn't change the reason I'm looking." Only say it if it is true; accepting counter-offers after an offer damages your reputation with recruiters.
</details>

<details>
<summary>"How do you handle stress?"</summary>

Give a concrete method (prioritised list, breaking work down, early communication) and a brief example, not "I don't get stressed".
</details>
